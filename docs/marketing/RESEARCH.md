# Deep Research — From Zero to First B2B Warehouse Customer

**Source:** TinyFish deep research, run 2026-11-25 (69 sources, 59 dated, latest 2026-11-17).
**Full report:** https://agent.tinyfish.ai/research/01a213bd-206e-760e-aadb-29ba2db54ce6
**Question:** Best step-by-step path for a solo dev with a working WMS, zero customers, selling a
one-time source-code buyout — from nothing to closing. With inputs/outputs for every step.

> Figures are market estimates from the cited industry sources; legal documents should be
> reviewed by a qualified software IP attorney before first use.

---

## Executive summary

B2B warehouse buyers are operationally conservative and evaluate via a committee of 6–10
stakeholders [22]. Software deals of $50K–$150K typically run 45–90+ days [39][66]. With zero
social proof, the path is to **compress risk at every stage**:
**positioning → cold outreach + LinkedIn → paid design-partner/pilot → reviews + case study +
referrals → full buyout.** The pilot _is_ the sales process for the first full deal.

Realistic timeline: **3–6 months to first signed buyout**; **first paid pilot in 4–8 weeks**.

---

## 1. Positioning: "Permanent Operational Control"

Cloud WMS is >75% of new deployments in 2026 [61], so a buyout swims against the tide — but a
defensible minority buys on-prem/perpetual for: data control [63][64], regulated/data-sovereignty
environments [60], unstable internet [63], deep workflow customization beyond APIs [64], and
5–7 year TCO [14][63].

Messaging pillars:

1. **You own it forever** — no escalation (SaaS commonly +7%/yr [59]), no lock-in.
2. **Deep workflow customization is built in** — adjust pipelines without API hacks.
3. **Continuity without a third party** — if the vendor disappears, the WMS still runs [26][32].
4. **One-time CapEx**, not OpEx — matters to some CFOs.

**Market pricing anchors:** perpetual WMS licenses $2,500–$200,000/facility mid-market [62],
enterprise $100,000–$500,000+ [3]; implementation adds $25,000–$75,000 (small site) to
$100,000–$350,000 [59]. Five-year SaaS TCO €48,500 vs on-prem €31,500 before IT/server costs,
converging to €45,000–55,000 [60].

**Buyout package must include (explicitly):** full repo with history [54], docs/runbook/schema/
API docs [32][54], agreed workflow customization, build instructions [52], defined migration
hours, training ($3,000–$5,000/team typical [14]), written IP assignment [58], 90-day support,
optional maintenance retainer at 15–20% of license/yr [2][14].
**Exclude explicitly:** third-party component licenses [32], future features, hosting.

## 2. Lead generation with zero audience

Inbound (SEO/content/ads) will not convert in time. Use direct/outbound/community.

### Cold email (primary)

- **Inputs:** 500–2,000 decision-makers (LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, ZoomInfo [43]); separate
  outreach domain; 2–3 warmed inboxes; 5-email sequence.
- **Outputs (2026 benchmarks):** personal reply 0.84%, positive reply 0.29%, ~1 booked call per
  871 contacts [4]; median campaign positive reply 0.11% [4]. At 1,000 contacts: 1–3 booked calls.
- **Who:** C-level (0.98% personal / 0.35% positive [4]), ops directors at 1–50 employee firms
  (7x reply vs enterprises [4]). Titles: Head of Operations, Supply Chain Director, Warehouse
  Manager, Head of IT [22]. Triggers: legacy-WMS pain, one site + growth, regulated data.
- **What converts:** narrow, operational pain + trigger, not features [22][44]; one hard outcome
  metric in email 2 [22]. Founder-led outreach beats SDR by 30–50% [28]. 3–5 emails, 3–5 days apart
  [28]; 58% of replies from email 1, 42% from follow-ups [18].
- **Timeline:** replies days 3–7; most logistics meetings from emails 3–5 [43]; 4–6 weeks per sequence.
- Logistics sales cycle from first meeting to contract: 2–6 months [43].

### LinkedIn (simultaneous)

- **Inputs:** Sales Navigator (~$100/mo); 20 personalized connection requests/week; InMail budget.
- **Outputs:** InMail response 18–25% [27]; combined with email lifts response well above either
  alone [22]. Expect 4–8 weeks to meaningful volume.
- Connect with a short note, no pitch; then conversation about operations.

### Directories (G2/Capterra/GetApp/Software Advice)

- One review system since G2's Feb 2026 acquisition: one approved review counts on all four
  sites where listed [21][31][6]. Capterra free-listing criteria: packaged software on public sale
  with demo/trial [57] — you qualify.
- **Almost no inbound until 10+ reviews [5]**; 1–2 reviews may surface in AI recommendations [5].
  Meaningful directory traffic: 3–6 months after 10+ reviews. **List now as foundation.**

### Communities

- **r/logistics:** self-promotion/interest links/service proposals banned; feedback in sticky
  thread only; needs 10 karma (5 from subreddit comments) [68]. Spend 4–6 weeks participating,
  then discuss the operational problem — do not pitch.
- **IWLA Convention & Expo** (2026: Mar 29–31, San Antonio) [69]: attend as delegate, talk to 3PLs.
- **warehouse-logistics.com** (Fraunhofer IML): ~20–25 qualified leads/month for listed providers,
  plus a Fraudhofer-audit validation seal; annual membership [36].
- LinkedIn/Slack groups: participate as practitioner.

### Partners/resellers

- Logistics consultants, ERP implementation partners, system integrators, 3PL consultants.
  Offer 10–15% of first-year contract value [34]. Find via LinkedIn and CSCMP/IWLA/TIA directories
  [43]. Takes 2–4 months; produces warm, fast-closing intros.

### Product Hunt

- **Skip.** Developer/tech audience; almost zero B2B warehouse-operator leads.

### 60-day priority

1. Cold email (500–1,000) → 1–3 calls; 2. LinkedIn 20/wk → 2–5 warm convos; 3. Free directory
   profiles → 0 now; 4. Communities → 0–2; 5. Partners → 0–1 intro.

## 3. First reviews with zero users

**Legitimate reviewers:** beta testers, pilot participants, former colleagues/contacts who
genuinely used it within 2 years. **Not:** your employees, competitors' staff, non-users, AI
reviews [6][5].

**Compliant path:** run a structured free/discounted 30–60 day pilot with 1–3 small warehouses;
these users become reviewers. Capterra allows ~$25 incentive; G2 up to $100 via managed campaigns
[5][31][57]; must be offered regardless of rating and disclosed. G2 Self-Service Review Campaigns
send up to 3 emails, bill only for approved reviews, ~3-day approval, 40-char minimum per section,
LinkedIn or corporate email required [47][5][6].

**Expect ~1% request→review conversion [5]** — ~100 outreach contacts per review; $250–$500 per
confirmed review. For executives: offer to draft the review for their approval [5].

**If prospect finds zero reviews:** "We're in early access — I can connect you with [pilot
participant] directly." A reference call is more credible than a review for the first sale [9][12].

**Minimum viable proof before first full sale:** 1 paid pilot, 1 reference contact, screenshots/
recording of the system running their operation, directory listings (even 0 reviews).

## 4. Discovery and demo

**Buying committee:** Warehouse Ops Manager/Head of Operations (pain owner), Head of IT (technical),
Finance/Procurement (budget), sometimes Supply Chain Director/COO [22][29]. Get both economic buyer
and daily user in the room early [16].

**Discovery (30–45 min, not a pitch):** understand current system, specific operational pain,
quantify it in their numbers, decision process/timeline/budget, constraints (internet, compliance,
integrations). Questions: "Walk me through truck arrival → putaway"; "Where do errors occur?";
"What systems do you use and their limits?"; "If fixed, what does that mean in hard numbers?";
"Biggest concern about implementation?"; "What must be true to move forward?" [9][12][29].

**Demo (45–60 min):** live software, never slides [13]. Restate their pain; show receiving →
inventory → directed picking → packing → shipping as one workflow; ask "does this match how your
team works?" at each step; show exception handling [9]; show relevant reports; explicitly show
configurability. Don't demo unasked features; don't discuss price [13]. **Send written recap within
24h** — deals with proposals sent within 24h close 35% faster [66].

**Cycle benchmarks:** <$10K → 20 days; $10K–$50K → 45 days; >$50K → 90 days [39]. Your
$25K–$80K buyout: plan 45–90 days from first contact to signature.

## 5. The SaaS objection: Acknowledge → Align → Advance [24]

1. **Acknowledge:** "Cloud SaaS is right for most warehouses; let's make sure we're a fit."
2. **Probe:** "What specifically drives wanting SaaS — upfront cost, no IT staff, maintenance,
   vendor risk?"
3. **Reframe:**
   - Upfront cost → 5-year TCO comparison; milestone payments.
   - No IT staff → optional **managed hosting add-on**; ownership separate from operations [58].
   - Maintenance → 90-day support + optional annual retainer; "pay for actual work, not access to
     your own data."
   - Vendor disappearance → "With SaaS, if vendor closes, your WMS is gone. You own this one."
   - Vendor risk → **source-code escrow bridge** (Codekeeper from $179/mo, Software-Escrow.com
     $89/mo, Escrow London $275/mo [56]) with release triggers [26][32].

**Qualify out** buyers who want fully managed cloud, have no IT, and are not regulated — the
45-day cycle is too expensive for misaligned prospects [39].

## 6. Paid pilot / design partner (the bridge to the close)

**Why paid:** free pilots attract disengaged users; a payment signals budget, alignment, intent [8]
[7][15][20]. Sierra's design partners all converted to paid; they required a real financial
commitment so buyers went through procurement [7].

- **Duration:** 60–90 days [46].
- **Scope:** one site, 1–2 workflows; anything else deferred [20][19].
- **Fee:** 10–30% of projected year-one value [19], $500 min; **credited in full** against buyout
  if converted within 30 days [46]. Illustrative: small $3K–$6K, mid $6K–$15K, larger $12K–$25K.
- **Success criteria (written, 2–4 metrics):** e.g. inventory accuracy, pick error rate, receiving
  time, reconciliation hours [20][17]. Auto-convert clause: "If 2 of 3 criteria met by Day 60,
  full buyout auto-converts unless buyer opts out in 7 days" [19].
- **Included:** config, setup, 60-day access, weekly 30-min check-ins [15][17], final before/after
  report.
- **Legal:** mutual NDA [12], pilot agreement/order form, 1–2 page SOW, data-handling terms [20].
- **Payment:** 50% upfront, 50% at Day 30 [8]. Above $10K expect 1–2 week procurement [19].

**Founding Partner program (first 2–3 buyers):** locked pilot price 24 months, roadmap council,
case study naming, 15–25% discount on buyout in exchange for a written case study and 1–3
reference calls [8]. Scarcity ("only 3 slots").

## 7. Pricing the buyout

| Tier               | SaaS/mo               | Perpetual license      | 5-yr SaaS TCO |
| ------------------ | --------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------- |
| Small (1–10 users) | $500–$3,000 [3]       | $2,500–$10,000 [14]    | $30K–$180K    |
| Mid (10–50 users)  | $2,000–$15,000 [2][3] | $30,000–$150,000 [2]   | $120K–$900K   |
| Enterprise         | $10,000–$50,000+      | $100,000–$500,000+ [3] | $600K–$3M+    |

**Your price:** base license $15K–$45K (single-site $15K–$25K; multi-site $25K–$50K), customization
$5K–$20K. Add-ons: annual maintenance 15–20% [2][14], extra customization $150–$250/hr, managed
hosting $200–$500/mo.

**Avoid underpricing:** price on buyer value, not time [10]; build ROI case during discovery [23];
anchor to 5-year SaaS alternative; pilot fee credited, never discounted; ask "what budget have you
identified?" before naming a number.

**Negotiation:** "lower price" → adjust scope instead; net-60 → milestone payments [59][3];
"upgrades forever" → covered by maintenance retainer; "what if you close" → escrow [26][32];
exclusive IP → 2–3x premium; standard buyout = internal use + modification rights. First customer
discount up to 20–25% framed as Founding Partner rate; never >30% [3].

## 8. Closing mechanics

- **Proposal within 24h of verbal yes** [66]; e-sign closes 60% faster [66]; winning proposals
  opened within 34 min [66]. Use DocuSign/PandaDoc/HelloSign; ESIGN-valid [52].
- **Proposal:** exec summary, scope (in/out), success criteria, itemized price, milestones,
  timeline, single CTA.
- **Contract package (attorney review $500–$2,000):** mutual NDA; **Source Code License and
  Assignment Agreement** with present-tense assignment ("assigns" not "will assign" [55]), precise
  license grant [48][52], third-party component list [32], background-IP retention [8], as-is
  warranty [48], liability cap at fees paid [52], IP indemnity [48], buyer confidentiality [52],
  governing law/arbitration; **SOW** with deliverables, milestones, acceptance, change-order
  process [20]; optional escrow.
- **Payment:** 30% signing / 40% go-live / 30% handover sign-off [59]. **Never deliver full source
  before final payment** unless contractually safe. Tie IP assignment to final payment [58].
- **Handover checklist [54][55]:** full repo with git history, architecture note/runbook, build
  instructions + dependency list, schema + migration scripts, all third-party licenses, credentials
  transferred, test docs, decision log, 2–3 walkthroughs. **Rebuild test:** buyer's IT deploys from
  delivered files alone before final payment — strongest verification of a complete handover.

## 9. Post-sale → referrals

- **Hypercare 4 weeks** post-go-live: defined response times, not unlimited [59].
- Weeks 1/2/4/8/12: install+train → first live transactions+daily check-in → review metrics →
  full handover → formal review + case study request.
- Avoid failure modes: bad data in, weak change management, big-bang go-live, weak support [51].
- **Case study within 60 days of go-live [19]:** before/after metrics on agreed criteria, quotes
  from ops manager and IT lead, workflow changes described. 1–2 page PDF + web page. Get publication
  rights in the pilot agreement [8].
- **Reviews:** ask at peak satisfaction [35][6]; G2 Self-Service Campaign; $25–$50 gift card;
  one G2 review counts on all four sites [21]. Ask everyone who touched the system [6].
- **Referrals:** activate after satisfaction; 10–15% of first-year value [34]; ask specifically
  ("who else runs [legacy WMS/spreadsheets]?"); referrals close 3–5x cold outbound [34]; 5–15% of
  customers refer, 25%+ convert to qualified opportunity [34][35].
- Also: industry event mention, co-presented webinar, LinkedIn recommendation, named case study
  in cold email touch 2 [22].

## 24-week roadmap

| Phase          | Weeks | Actions                                                                  | Success metric            |
| -------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Foundation     | 1–2   | Offer doc; G2/Capterra listings; 500–1,000 contact list; outreach domain | Listings live, list ready |
| Outreach       | 3–6   | Cold email sequence; LinkedIn 20/wk; communities                         | 1–3 discovery calls       |
| Pilot close    | 6–10  | Discovery → demo → pilot proposal; NDA + agreement; fee                  | First paid pilot $3K–$8K  |
| Pilot delivery | 10–18 | Weekly check-ins; live in warehouse; metrics tracked                     | Criteria met              |
| Buyout         | 16–20 | Auto-convert or proposal; negotiation; milestones                        | First buyout $20K–$60K    |
| Proof          | 18–24 | Case study; reviews; referral program; second prospect                   | 3–5 reviews, 1 referral   |

## Risk flags

Buyer won't pay pilot → success-gated LOI alternative, never fully free [8]; no reviews → build
commitment into pilot agreement + incentives; SaaS insistence → managed hosting bridge, qualify
out; IP dispute → present-tense assignment, payment tied to delivery; underpricing → ROI before
price; scope creep → 3-outcome SOW + change orders [19][8]; procurement → 1–2 week window, Mutual
Action Plan [41].
